The StoryBrand framework, in plain words

StoryBrand — often called the SB7 — is a seven-part framework for telling a clear story. Here it is, part by part, in everyday language.
Part 1 · A character

Your customer is the hero

Every clear story starts with a character who wants something. In your story, that character is your customer, your coaching client, or the people your nonprofit serves — never your business itself.

Part 2 · A problem

The problem they are facing

The hero has a problem with three layers: the outside problem everyone can see, the inside feeling it causes, and the bigger reason it is not right. Name all three and your hero feels understood.

Part 3 · A guide

You are the guide, not the hero

The hero is not looking for another hero. They are looking for a guide: someone who understands what they are facing and has helped people through it before. That is you, your business, your coaching, or your nonprofit.

Part 4 · A plan

A plan with simple steps

The guide gives the hero a plan: a few clear steps that make starting feel possible. When the next step is simple, people take it. When it is confusing, they walk away.

Part 5 · A call to action

One clear ask

Heroes do not act unless they are called to act. Your story asks plainly — buy, book, give, join — and offers a smaller first step for people who are not ready yet.

Part 6 · Failure

What happens if they do nothing

Every story has stakes. Said honestly, never as a scare tactic: what does it cost your hero if the problem stays exactly as it is? Stakes are what make a story matter.

Part 7 · Success

What life looks like when it works

End the story by painting the picture: where your hero lands when the problem is solved. A customer’s easier day, a coaching client’s real change, a community your nonprofit helped flourish. Success is the picture your hero is walking toward through all seven parts.

The usual story vs The StoryBrand story

Most marketing tells the wrong story. StoryBrand turns it around, and the prompt is built to keep your story turned the right way.

The usual story

Your business is the hero. Your history, your awards, your features, your years in business. Your customer listens politely — and wonders what any of it has to do with them.

The StoryBrand story

Your customer is the hero, facing a problem they can name. You step in as the guide who understands them, hands them a simple plan, and calls them to act — so they avoid failure and reach success.

Why it works

People see themselves in the hero’s place. When your customer can tell, in one breath, what you do, who it helps, and what changes, they remember it, repeat it, and act on it.

What this Muse prompt does

One prompt, three jobs, done with you from start to finish: Muse finds your customer’s own words, builds your story with you in StoryBrand order, and shows it to you for review before anything is shared.

It searches

Muse goes and looks first, on Facebook and Instagram and then the web, for the words your customers, clients, and donors actually use, instead of inventing marketing language for you.

It teaches by building

Muse explains each of the seven StoryBrand parts in plain words, then builds that part of your story with you, so you learn the framework by using it, not by studying it.

It keeps you the guide

Muse checks your story as it goes: is your customer still the hero? Is the plan simple? Is the ask clear? Then it shows you the finished story for review. Nothing goes out without your approval.

Learn the framework, never copy a story. Muse studies what your customers say and how clear stories are shaped. It does not copy anyone’s words, photos, or story, and your finished story is built only from your own offer and your own voice.

The prompt

Copy it, paste it into Muse, and fill in the blank with what you offer and who you serve. Muse will ask you any questions it needs before it starts.

Please ensure that your Meta Muse account is connected to your FB/IG

Based on [ what I offer and who I serve ], help me learn StoryBrand storytelling with Muse.

Before you start, please say to me: "Please ensure that your Facebook or Instagram is connected to Muse first." If it is not connected yet, ask me to connect it, or to share my posts with you, before you search Facebook and Instagram.

Use the StoryBrand framework created by Donald Miller, author of Building a StoryBrand. Teach me each part in plain words as we build, in the framework's own order. Do not copy text from the book.

First, search Facebook and Instagram first, then the web, for the words my customers, clients, donors, or community actually use about the problem I help with. Use public material, and material I share with you.

Then help me build my story in the seven StoryBrand parts:
1. A character: my customer is the hero, not me.
2. A problem: name the outside problem, the inside feeling it causes, and why it is not right.
3. A guide: that is me. Show that I understand them and that I can help.
4. A plan: a few simple steps they can follow.
5. A call to action: one clear ask, and a smaller first step for people who are not ready yet.
6. Failure: what it costs them if they do nothing, said honestly.
7. Success: what life looks like when it works.

As we build each part, check my story with me: is my customer still the hero, and am I the guide?

Write my story in my own words, so it sounds like me, and show it to me for review before I share it.

Do not post or send anything without my approval.

Ask me any questions you may have before you start.

The blank is yours to fill: [ what I offer and who I serve ]. Everything else stays exactly as written. Use it again whenever your message needs to get clearer: a new offer, a new program, a fundraising appeal, or a homepage rewrite. One prompt, every story.

For small business owners, coaches & nonprofit owners

  • Small business owners: people like you, but they cannot tell in one breath what you do or why it matters, so they move on.
  • Coaches: your results are real, but your message sounds like everyone else’s, and the change you lead is hard to explain clearly.
  • Nonprofit owners & leaders: your mission matters deeply, but your appeals talk about your organization more than the people you serve.
  • You know your story could be clearer, and you want a framework you can reuse, not a one-time rewrite.

What it does not do

It does not read private comments, direct messages, or private groups, and it does not promise an audience or sales. No honest tool can. What it does is study public words with you, teach you the StoryBrand framework in plain words, and help you build your clear story on it, with the final say still yours.
